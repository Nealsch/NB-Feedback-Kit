# About Me

**The person behind NB Feedback Kit.**

</div>

---

Hi, I'm Neal.

I'm not a software developer.

At least, that's what I told myself for most of my life.

I've spent much of my career living in the space between business and technology, acting as an interpreter between people with ideas and people with the skills to build them. I learned how to translate requirements into solutions, explain technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders, and help both sides understand each other.

But I've always admired developers.

To me, developers are creators.

They take an idea that exists only in someone's imagination and turn it into something real. Something living. Something that solves problems, creates value, and reaches people all over the world.

I've always been fascinated by the foundations that make it possible. The logic. The mathematics. The elegance of hardware. Logic gates. Processors. Networks. The incredible reality that billions of ones and zeros can be orchestrated to perform meaningful work in the blink of an eye.

I loved it all.

I just never truly believed I could be one of the people creating it.

Then AI happened.

Large language models and modern AI development tools changed something for me. For the first time, I felt like I could assemble my own team. I could collaborate with specialists, ask questions without embarrassment, learn by doing, and transform ideas into working software.

Not because AI replaced developers.

Quite the opposite.

It gave me a deeper appreciation for what developers do every day and allowed me to participate in the process in a way I never thought possible.

I finally stopped standing next to the workshop and stepped inside.

NB Feedback Kit is one of the first products born from that decision.

It represents many things I care about:

- Ownership over your own tools and data.
- Thoughtful architecture.
- Security by design.
- Simplicity where possible.
- Structure with purpose.
- Building useful things that solve real problems.

More than anything, though, it represents a willingness to create publicly.

That is surprisingly intimidating.

Developers put a part of themselves into their work. Every design choice, every architecture decision, every compromise and improvement leaves a fingerprint. Software is technical, but it is also deeply creative.

This project carries some of mine.

So here it is.

My first serious attempt to contribute something back to a community I have admired for a very long time.

I know there will be mistakes.

I know there will be things that can be improved.

And I know there are people who have forgotten more about software engineering than I have learned so far.

But every project starts somewhere.

I hope you'll judge the work fairly, challenge the ideas constructively, and help me make it better.

If this project proves useful to even a handful of developers, I'll consider it a success.

And if I'm fortunate, this will be the first of many things I build and share with the world.
